One word, one rule, no exceptions:

> **ojalá is always followed by the subjunctive.**

- `**Ojalá llueva.**` — I hope it rains.
- `**Ojalá sea** más fácil.` — I hope it's easier.
- `**Ojalá pueda** ir.` — I hope I can go.

There is nothing to decide. Unlike `querer que`, it does not care how many subjects there are —
`Ojalá siga estudiando` is about the speaker herself and still takes the subjunctive.

## Where it comes from

`ojalá` entered Spanish from Arabic, from a phrase meaning roughly **"if God wills"** — the same
expression that survives elsewhere as *inshallah*. Eight centuries of Arabic presence in Iberia
left several thousand words in Spanish, and this is among the most used.

Knowing the origin helps the grammar make sense: it was never a statement about the world, only
ever a wish laid at someone else's feet. That is exactly what the subjunctive is for.

## Translating it

English has no single equivalent, so it comes out differently depending on context:

| | |
|---|---|
| `Ojalá llueva.` | I hope it rains. |
| `Ojalá estuviera aquí.` | If only she were here. |
| `¡Ojalá!` | I wish! / If only! |

On its own, `¡Ojalá!` is a complete answer — roughly "don't I wish".

## Watch out

`ojalá que` also exists and means the same thing; the `que` is optional and slightly more
common in Latin America than in Spain. Both are correct:

> `Ojalá **que** llueva.` = `Ojalá llueva.`

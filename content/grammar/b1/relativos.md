Relative pronouns join two sentences into one, replacing a repeated noun.

> `Tengo una vecina. La vecina toca el piano.`
> → `Tengo una vecina **que** toca el piano.`

## que does almost everything

**que** covers who, whom, that and which. It never changes for gender or number, and it works for
people and things alike. Roughly nine times in ten it is the right answer.

- `La chica **que** vive arriba` — a person
- `La única cosa **que** me dice que es domingo` — a thing
- `Un acuerdo **que** nadie ha firmado` — after a plural or singular alike

## After a preposition, que needs help

| | |
|---|---|
| people | **quien** / **el que** / **la que** |
| things | **el que** / **la que** / **los que** |

- `La persona con **quien** hablé.`
- `El departamento en **el que** nadie contestó.`

The article agrees with the noun: `el departamento` → `en **el** que`.

English drops the relative entirely here — "the person I spoke to" — which is why learners
forget it. Spanish never drops it.

## lo que: a whole idea

Use **lo que** when what you are referring to is not a noun but an entire thought:

- `**Lo que** me molesta es la sensación.`
- `No entendí **lo que** dijiste.`
- `**Lo que** he aprendido no cabe en una lista.`

`lo cual` does the same job when referring back to a whole previous clause: `Dijo que me echaba
de menos, **lo cual** me pareció generoso`.

## cuyo: whose

Rare in speech, common in writing. It agrees with **what is owned**, not with the owner:

> `Una vecina **cuyo hijo** es médico.`

The neighbour is female; `cuyo` is masculine because `hijo` is. Likewise `cuya hija`,
`cuyos hijos`.

## The comma changes the meaning

| | |
|---|---|
| `Mi hermana **que** vive en Sevilla` | I have several sisters; this is the Seville one |
| `Mi hermana, **que** vive en Sevilla,` | I have one sister, and by the way she lives in Seville |

Without commas the clause identifies which one. With commas it merely adds information. English
marks this the same way, and most English speakers do it by instinct without knowing the rule.

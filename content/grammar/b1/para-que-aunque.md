Two conjunctions worth separating out: one that always takes the subjunctive, and one that takes
either — with a change of meaning.

## para que: always subjunctive

> `Le escribo **para que me explique** una cosa.`

A purpose is by definition not yet achieved, so there is nothing to decide. `para que` takes the
subjunctive one hundred per cent of the time.

Others in the same family: **a fin de que**, **con el fin de que**, **de modo que** (when it
means "so that").

**One subject means no `que`.** This is the same test as everywhere else, and here it maps onto
the `por`/`para` distinction from Unit 9:

| | |
|---|---|
| `Estudio **para viajar**.` | I study, I travel |
| `Te lo digo **para que** no **esperes**.` | I tell, you wait |

## aunque: both moods, two meanings

This is the interesting one.

| | |
|---|---|
| `**Aunque llueve**, vamos.` | Even **though** it is raining — a fact I'm conceding |
| `**Aunque llueva**, vamos.` | Even **if** it rains — a possibility I'm allowing for |

English uses two different phrases — "even though" and "even if" — where Spanish changes only the
mood. Both Spanish sentences are correct; they say different things.

The test: **do you know it to be true?**

- It is raining right now, and I am acknowledging that → `aunque llueve`
- It might rain on Saturday, and I am covering for it → `aunque llueva`

The same works for `a pesar de que`.

## Also in this family

**mientras** changes meaning as well as mood:

| | |
|---|---|
| `**Mientras** espero, leo.` | while — time |
| `**Mientras** haya café, estaré bien.` | as long as — condition |

Time takes the indicative; condition takes the subjunctive.

## Watch out

`aun` (no accent) means "even" and belongs to `aun así` — even so. `aún` (with an accent) means
"still" or "yet". They sound almost identical and mean different things: `**aún** no ha llegado`
is "he hasn't arrived yet".

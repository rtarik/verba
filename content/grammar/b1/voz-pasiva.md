Spanish has a passive built exactly like English's — **ser** + participle — and uses it far less.

> `El edificio **fue construido** en mil novecientos veinte.`

The participle behaves like an adjective and **agrees with the subject**:

- `El acuerdo fue firm**ado**.`
- `La novela fue escrit**a**.`
- `Las cartas fueron enviad**as**.`

To name who did it, use `por`: `La novela fue escrita **por** su hermana.`

## It sounds formal

This construction belongs to newspapers, reports and legal documents. In conversation it sounds
stiff or translated. Spanish has two everyday alternatives, and both are more natural:

| | |
|---|---|
| `El edificio **fue construido** en 1920.` | formal, written |
| `**Se construyó** en 1920.` | the impersonal `se` — Unit 15 |
| `**Lo construyeron** en 1920.` | an unspecified "they" |

All three say the same thing. The third is what a Spanish speaker would actually say.

## Why this matters for learners

English uses the passive constantly, so translating word for word produces Spanish that is
grammatically correct and audibly foreign. If you find yourself writing `fue` + participle
repeatedly, reach for `se` or an unspecified plural instead.

This is one of the clearest cases where the right answer is not a rule but a habit — and the
habit comes from reading, not from grammar.

## ser or estar + participle?

Both exist and they mean different things:

| | |
|---|---|
| `La puerta **fue cerrada** a las diez.` | the action — someone closed it |
| `La puerta **estaba cerrada**.` | the resulting state — it was shut |

`ser` describes the event; `estar` describes the condition it left behind. The `ser`/`estar`
split from Unit 1 reaching all the way up here.

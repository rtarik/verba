When you repeat what someone said, the tenses **step one pace backwards**.

| they said | you report |
|---|---|
| «Estoy bien» | Dijo que **estaba** bien |
| «He hablado» | Dijo que **había hablado** |
| «Hablé ayer» | Dijo que **había hablado** ayer |
| «Iré» | Dijo que **vendría** |
| «Iría» | Dijo que **iría** *(no change)* |

The pattern:

- present → **imperfecto**
- perfect and preterite → **pluscuamperfecto**
- future → **conditional**
- imperfecto and conditional → unchanged, they are already as far back as it goes

English does exactly the same thing — *"I'm fine"* becomes *"he said he was fine"* — so the
instinct is already there. Spanish is just more consistent about it.

## Only after a past reporting verb

If the reporting verb is present or perfect, nothing shifts:

> `**Dice** que **está** bien.` — no change
> `**Ha dicho** que **vendrá**.` — no change

The backward step happens because `dijo` is itself in the past.

## Reported questions

The question marks disappear and a linking word appears:

| | |
|---|---|
| `¿Quieres?` | Me preguntó **si** quería. |
| `¿Cuánto tiempo llevas?` | Me preguntó **cuánto tiempo** llevaba. |

For yes/no questions the link is **si** — here meaning "whether", not "if". Question words keep
their accents even with no question marks around them: `me preguntó **cuánto**`,
`me preguntó **dónde**`.

## Reported orders take the subjunctive

An instruction becomes `que` + subjunctive — the same structure as `pedir que` from Unit 11:

| | |
|---|---|
| `¡Piénsalo!` | Me pide **que lo piense**. |
| `¡Ven!` | Me dice **que venga**. |

## Watch out

`decir que` still splits two ways, as in Unit 11. `Dijo que **venía**` reports a fact he stated;
`Dijo que **viniera**` reports an order he gave. The second uses the past subjunctive, which
arrives in Unit 17.

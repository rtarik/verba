Unit 11 covered wanting. This is the second trigger, and the one with the sharpest test:

> **Assert it → indicative. Doubt or deny it → subjunctive.**

| | |
|---|---|
| `Creo que **es** difícil.` | I'm asserting it |
| `No creo que **sea** difícil.` | I'm not |

Nothing moved except the word `no`. That is the whole mechanism.

## The asymmetry

This is what makes the rule feel strange at first: the *same verb* takes different moods
depending on whether it is negated.

| affirmative → indicative | negative → subjunctive |
|---|---|
| Creo que **viene**. | No creo que **venga**. |
| Es verdad que **puede**. | No es verdad que **pueda**. |
| Está claro que **sabe**. | No está claro que **sepa**. |
| Estoy segura de que **aprueba**. | No estoy segura de que **apruebe**. |

Asserting presents a fact. Denying turns the fact back into a possibility — and possibilities
are subjunctive territory.

## Verbs that always take it

Some expressions carry doubt built in, so they take the subjunctive whether or not there is a
`no`:

| | |
|---|---|
| **dudar que** | `Dudo que llegue a tiempo.` |
| **no es que** | `No es que sea irresponsable.` |
| **es posible que** | `Es posible que pierda la noción del tiempo.` |
| **puede que** | `Puede que venga.` |

`No es que` is worth noticing: it raises an idea specifically in order to reject it, so it is
doubtful by construction.

## quizá, tal vez, a lo mejor

These three behave differently, and the difference is real:

- **quizá / tal vez** take the subjunctive when you are genuinely unsure, the indicative when you
  are fairly confident. `Quizás **esté** en el metro` (guessing) against `Quizás **está** en el
  metro` (probably is).
- **a lo mejor** always takes the indicative, despite meaning the same thing. `A lo mejor
  **viene**.`

## Watch out

Questions with `creer` flip the logic. Because a question does not assert anything, the
subjunctive becomes available:

> `¿Crees que **venga**?` — do you think he'll come? *(I have my doubts)*
> `¿Crees que **viene**?` — do you think he's coming? *(a neutral question)*

Both are correct. The subjunctive version quietly signals that you are sceptical.

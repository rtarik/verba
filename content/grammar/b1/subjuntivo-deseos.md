Knowing how to build the subjunctive is the easy half. Knowing when it appears is the real work,
and this unit covers the clearest trigger: **one person wanting something of another**.

## Count the subjects

> **One subject → infinitive. Two subjects → que + subjunctive.**

| | |
|---|---|
| `Quiero **ir** a Sevilla.` | I want, I go. One person. |
| `Quiero **que vengas** a Sevilla.` | I want, you come. Two people. |

This is the rule the whole unit turns on. English blurs it — *"I want to go"* and *"I want you to
go"* both use an infinitive — so English speakers reach for `Quiero tú ir`, which is impossible.
Spanish needs `que`, and after `que` the verb changes mood.

## The verbs that trigger it

Anything expressing a wish, a hope, a request or an instruction:

| | |
|---|---|
| **querer que** | `Quiero que vengas.` |
| **esperar que** | `Espero que todo salga bien.` |
| **preferir que** | `Prefiero que vayas a trabajar.` |
| **pedir que** | `Me ha pedido que llame a los clientes.` |
| **decir que** | `Dile que venga.` |
| **necesitar que** | `Necesito que me ayudes.` |

## decir is two verbs in one

`decir que` behaves differently depending on what you mean:

| | |
|---|---|
| `Dile que **viene**.` | Tell him that he's coming — reporting a fact |
| `Dile que **venga**.` | Tell him to come — an instruction |

Indicative reports; subjunctive instructs. Same words, and only the mood separates them. This is
the clearest evidence that the subjunctive carries real meaning rather than being decoration.

## que + subjunctive on its own

Dropped in front of a sentence with no main verb, it becomes a third-person command:

- `**Que se enfade** si quiere.` — Let her be annoyed if she likes.
- `**Que tengas** un buen día.` — Have a good day. *(literally: may you have)*

## Watch out

The trigger must be a genuine wish. Verbs of **thinking and knowing** take the indicative:

- `Creo que **es** difícil.` — I think it is
- `Sé que **viene**.` — I know he's coming

Negate them, though, and doubt creeps in — `No creo que **sea** difícil` — which is where
Unit 12 begins.

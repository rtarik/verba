Spanish possessives are simpler than English ones in one important way: they do **not** change
according to who owns the thing. `mi` is "my" whether the thing is masculine or feminine.

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| **mi** casa | my house |
| **tu** familia | your family |
| **su** gato | his / her cat |

## The only change is singular vs plural

What they do change for is *how many things there are* — not their gender.

- **mi** amigo → **mis** amigos
- **tu** gato → **tus** gatos

So it is `mis amigos` because there are several friends, not because of anything about *me*.

## The accent that changes the word

These two look almost identical and mean different things:

| | |
|---|---|
| **tu** | your — `¿Y tu familia?` |
| **tú** | you — `¿Y tú?` |

Both appear in *Mi familia*, one line apart. The accent is the only difference, and it is not
optional.

## A note on su

`su` covers "his", "her", "its", and "their" all at once. Spanish usually lets context do the
work, and only spells it out when there would be real confusion.

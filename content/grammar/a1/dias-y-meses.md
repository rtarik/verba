**No capital letters.** Neither days nor months are capitalised in Spanish — `lunes`, not
*Lunes*. This is one of the most persistent English habits to unlearn.

## The article does the work

Where English needs "on", Spanish uses **el** or **los**:

| | |
|---|---|
| `**El** lunes tengo una reunión.` | on Monday — one specific Monday |
| `**Los** lunes trabajo en casa.` | on Mondays — every week |

That single article is the difference between a one-off and a habit, so it carries real meaning.

Five of the seven days already end in `-s` and do not change in the plural: `los lunes`,
`los martes`. Only `sábado` and `domingo` add one: `los sábados`, `los domingos`.

## Months

enero, febrero, marzo, abril, mayo, junio, julio, agosto, septiembre, octubre, noviembre,
diciembre.

Also lower case. With months, Spanish uses **en**: `**en** julio`, `**en** septiembre`.

## Dates

> `el **quince** de marzo` — the fifteenth of March

Cardinal numbers throughout — `el dos`, `el quince`, `el treinta` — not ordinals. The only
exception is the first, which may be `el **primero** de marzo` or simply `el uno de marzo`.

Years are read as whole numbers: `mil novecientos sesenta`, never as two pairs.

## Watch out

`el fin de semana` is singular in Spanish where English says "the weekend"; the plural
`los fines de semana` means "at weekends", habitually. Same article rule as the days.

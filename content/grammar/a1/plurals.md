The rule is short: **add -s after a vowel, add -es after a consonant.**

| singular | plural | |
|---|---|---|
| gato | gato**s** | ends in a vowel |
| amigo | amigo**s** | ends in a vowel |
| ciudad | ciudad**es** | ends in a consonant |
| país | país**es** | ends in a consonant |

## Everything around the noun has to follow

This is the part that takes practice. When the noun goes plural, the article *and* every
adjective go plural too:

- `un gato pequeño` → `dos gato**s** pequeño**s**`
- `el gato blanco` → `**los** gato**s** blanco**s**`

You can see all three words changing together in *Mis amigos*: `Los gatos son blancos`.

## The articles

| | singular | plural |
|---|---|---|
| masculine | el | **los** |
| feminine | la | **las** |
| "a" / "some" | un / una | **unos** / **unas** |

## Watch out

`país` loses its accent in the plural: `país` → `paises`… almost. It is written **países** —
the accent stays because it is still needed to mark the stress. Accents in Spanish track
pronunciation, so they move or vanish when the shape of the word changes.

Spanish has two verbs for "to be". You already know **ser**. This is **estar**, and mixing them
up is the single most common beginner mistake — worth getting straight early.

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| **estoy** | I am |
| **estás** | you are |
| **está** | he/she/it is |

## The difference in one line

**ser** is what something *is*. **estar** is how something *is doing*.

| | |
|---|---|
| `Ana **es** simpática` | Ana is friendly — that is what she is like, always |
| `Ana **está** cansada` | Ana is tired — today, right now |

Both sentences are correct, and swapping the verbs would change the meaning rather than simply
sounding wrong. `Ana es cansada` would suggest being tiresome is part of her personality.

## What estar covers

- **How you feel**: `Estoy cansada.` `¿Qué tal estás?`
- **Where something is**: `Madrid está en España.`

## A memory hook that actually holds

If the description could change by tomorrow, reach for **estar**. If it would still be true next
year, reach for **ser**.

Feelings change, so they take *estar*. Where you are from does not, so it takes *ser* — which is
why *¿De dónde eres?* uses **eres** and not **estás**.

Spanish verbs come in three families, named after the last two letters of their dictionary form:
**-ar**, **-er** and **-ir**. To use one, you chop off those two letters and add an ending that
says *who* is doing it.

## The endings

| | hablar (-ar) | comer (-er) | escribir (-ir) |
|---|---|---|---|
| yo | habl**o** | com**o** | escrib**o** |
| tú | habl**as** | com**es** | escrib**es** |
| él / ella | habl**a** | com**e** | escrib**e** |
| nosotros | habl**amos** | com**emos** | escrib**imos** |
| ellos | habl**an** | com**en** | escrib**en** |

## Two things worth noticing

**The -er and -ir families are nearly identical.** Look at the two right-hand columns: they only
differ in the *nosotros* row (com**emos** vs escrib**imos**). Everywhere else they match. So you
are really learning two sets of endings, not three.

**The `yo` form always ends in -o.** All three families. `hablo`, `como`, `escribo`, `trabajo`,
`bebo`, `leo`. This is the single most reliable pattern in the language, and it is why you can
often guess the *I* form of a verb you have never seen.

## Why you rarely need the pronoun

Because the ending already identifies the subject, `hablo` on its own means "I speak". Adding
`yo` is optional and adds emphasis — see [subject pronouns](subject-pronouns).

## Watch out: verbs that break in the middle

Some very common verbs change their stem when the stress falls on it:

- `empezar` → `emp**ie**zo` (not "empezo")
- `volver` → `v**ue**lvo` (not "volvo")
- `dormir` → `d**ue**rmo` (not "dormo")

The *endings* stay perfectly regular — it is only the middle of the word that shifts. And it
only happens when that syllable is stressed, which is why `nosotros` keeps the plain stem:
`volvemos`, not "vuelvemos".

`ir` ("to go") is in a class of its own: `voy`, `vas`, `va`. Nothing about it is predictable, so
learn it as a set.

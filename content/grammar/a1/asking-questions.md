## The question words

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| **qué** | what |
| **quién** | who |
| **dónde** | where |
| **cuándo** | when |
| **cómo** | how |
| **cuánto** | how much |
| **por qué** | why |

Every one of them carries an accent. That accent is what marks the word as a question — the same
words without it are ordinary connectors, which is why `porque` (because) and `por qué` (why)
are different things.

## Two punctuation marks, not one

Spanish opens a question with an upside-down **¿** and closes it with **?**:

> `¿Dónde está el museo?`

It is not decoration. Spanish word order barely changes for questions, so without the opening
mark you would read half the sentence before discovering it was a question at all. The `¿` warns
you in advance — and it goes exactly where the question starts, which is not always the start of
the sentence:

> `Perdón, ¿dónde está el museo?`

## Questions without a question word

Just wrap a statement in the marks. The words need not move:

- `El museo está cerca.` → `¿El museo está cerca?`

Spoken, it is your voice that rises. Written, the `¿ ?` do the work. This is why Spanish
speakers learning English often forget to invert the English word order — their own language
never asked them to.

## cuánto changes shape

Unlike the others, `cuánto` behaves like an adjective and agrees with what it counts:

- `¿**Cuánto** cuesta?` — How much does it cost?
- `¿**Cuántos** años tienes?` — How many years do you have? *(masculine plural, for años)*
- `¿**Cuántas** tiendas hay?` — How many shops are there? *(feminine plural)*

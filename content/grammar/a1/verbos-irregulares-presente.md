A large group of very common verbs are regular in their endings but change a vowel **in the
middle** — and only when that syllable is stressed.

## The three patterns

| change | example | |
|---|---|---|
| **e → ie** | querer → qu**ie**ro | pensar, empezar, entender, preferir, sentir |
| **o → ue** | poder → p**ue**do | volver, dormir, encontrar, costar, recordar |
| **e → i** | pedir → p**i**do | seguir, repetir, servir, decir, reír |

## The boot

The change happens in four of the five forms, and skips `nosotros`:

| | querer | poder | pedir |
|---|---|---|---|
| yo | qu**ie**ro | p**ue**do | p**i**do |
| tú | qu**ie**res | p**ue**des | p**i**des |
| él / ella | qu**ie**re | p**ue**de | p**i**de |
| **nosotros** | **queremos** | **podemos** | **pedimos** |
| ellos | qu**ie**ren | p**ue**den | p**i**den |

Draw a line around the forms that change and you get a shape like a boot — which is what teachers
usually call it. The reason is stress: in `queremos` the stress falls on the ending, so the stem
vowel stays quiet.

## Why it matters beyond the present

These same changes reappear later, so learning them once pays off repeatedly:

- **gerunds**: dormir → d**u**rmiendo, decir → d**i**ciendo, pedir → p**i**diendo
- **preterite** (he/she and they forms): dormir → d**u**rmió, pedir → p**i**dió
- **present subjunctive**: querer → qu**ie**ra, poder → p**ue**da

## Watch out

The infinitive gives no warning. `Contar` and `cortar` look alike; `contar` changes
(`c**ue**nto`) and `cortar` does not (`corto`). There is no rule — the change is a property of
each verb, learned with it.

Verbs with an irregular `yo` form (`tengo`, `salgo`, `hago`, `conozco`) are a separate matter,
and they carry through to [the subjunctive](subjuntivo-presente).

Both translate as "very" or "a lot", and choosing wrongly is one of the most common beginner
mistakes. The rule is mechanical:

> **muy** goes with adjectives and adverbs. **mucho** goes with nouns and verbs.

| | |
|---|---|
| `Es **muy** grande.` | + adjective |
| `Habla **muy** despacio.` | + adverb |
| `Tiene **mucho** dinero.` | + noun |
| `Trabaja **mucho**.` | + verb |

`Muy` never changes. `Mucho` changes when it describes a noun: `much**a** gente`,
`much**os** estudiantes`, `much**as** tiendas`.

## The test

Ask what the word is attached to.

- describing a *quality* → **muy**
- describing an *amount* → **mucho**

`Es muy difícil` (how difficult) against `Hay mucha dificultad` (how much difficulty).

## Never together

`Muy mucho` does not exist. To intensify `mucho`, use **muchísimo**:

> `Me gusta **muchísimo**.`

That `-ísimo` ending works on adjectives too, and is very common in speech: `grandísimo`,
`buenísimo`, `carísimo`, `aburridísimo`.

## Watch out

Two fixed exceptions where English would push you towards `muy`:

| | |
|---|---|
| `**mucho** mejor` | much better |
| `**mucho** peor` | much worse |

Also `mucho más` and `mucho menos`. These are adverbs, but they take `mucho` — think of it as
"much", not "very", and they stop looking odd.

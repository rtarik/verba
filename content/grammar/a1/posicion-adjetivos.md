The default in Spanish is the opposite of English: **the adjective follows the noun.**

- `un gato **negro**` — a black cat
- `una ciudad **grande**` — a big city
- `un restaurante **pequeño**` — a small restaurant

Adjectives that classify — colour, shape, nationality, type — essentially always go after.

## Which ones go in front

A small set of very common adjectives normally precede the noun:

| | |
|---|---|
| **bueno / malo** | `un **buen** amigo` |
| **grande** | `una **gran** ciudad` |
| **primero / último** | `el **primer** día` |
| **mucho / poco** | `**mucha** gente` |
| **otro** | `**otro** día` |

Note the shortening: `bueno`, `malo`, `primero` and `tercero` drop their final `-o` before a
masculine singular noun — `buen`, `mal`, `primer`, `tercer`. `Grande` shortens to `gran` before
either gender.

## Position can change the meaning

With a handful of adjectives, moving it changes what you are saying:

| before | after |
|---|---|
| `un **gran** hombre` — a great man | `un hombre **grande**` — a big man |
| `un **viejo** amigo` — a long-standing friend | `un amigo **viejo**` — an elderly friend |
| `**cierta** información` — certain (some) information | `información **cierta**` — true information |
| `mi **antiguo** jefe` — my former boss | `un edificio **antiguo**` — an ancient building |

The pattern: **before** the noun tends toward the subjective or figurative; **after** the noun
states a plain fact.

## Watch out

Putting a descriptive adjective in front is not wrong — it is a stylistic choice that sounds
literary or emotional. `La blanca nieve` is perfectly grammatical; it just sounds like poetry
rather than a weather report. Beginners are safest defaulting to noun-then-adjective.

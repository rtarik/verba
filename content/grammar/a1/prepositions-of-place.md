| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| **en** | in, on |
| **cerca de** | near |
| **lejos de** | far from |
| **al lado de** | next to |
| **detrás de** | behind |
| **debajo de** | under |
| **entre** | between |

## Notice the 'de'

Most of these end in **de**, and it is not optional:

- `cerca **de** la universidad` — near the university
- `al lado **de** mi casa` — next to my house

Drop the `de` and the phrase only works when nothing follows it. Compare:

- `El parque está lejos.` — The park is far away. *(nothing follows, so no de)*
- `El parque está lejos **de** mi casa.` — far from my house. *(something follows, so de)*

That is the whole rule: **de** appears when you name the thing you are measuring from.

## The contraction trap

`de` + `el` is always written **del**, never `de el`:

- `cerca **del** centro` — near the centre
- `detrás **del** banco` — behind the bank

But only before `el`. It stays separate before the others: `cerca de la plaza`,
`cerca de los árboles`.

This is the same squashing you saw with `a` + `el` = `al` in Unit 2. Spanish does it in exactly
two places, and these are both of them.

## Which verb goes with them

Almost always **estar**, because position is a circumstance rather than an identity — see
[the other 'to be': estar](estar-basics). The exception is when you are introducing something
rather than locating it, and then you want [hay](hay-vs-estar).

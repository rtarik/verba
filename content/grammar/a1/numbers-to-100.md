## The tens

| | | | |
|---|---|---|---|
| diez 10 | veinte 20 | treinta 30 | cuarenta 40 |
| cincuenta 50 | sesenta 60 | setenta 70 | ochenta 80 |
| noventa 90 | cien 100 | | |

From forty upward, most tens simply take the matching single digit and add **-enta**:
cuatro → cuar**enta**, seis → ses**enta**, siete → set**enta**, ocho → och**enta**,
nueve → nov**enta**.

## Building the numbers in between

From thirty on, join the ten and the digit with **y**:

- treinta **y** uno — 31
- cuarenta **y** dos — 42
- setenta **y** cinco — 75

The twenties are the exception: they fuse into a single word with no `y` — veintiuno,
veintidós, veinticinco. Just the twenties; everything above behaves.

## Where you meet them

- **Street numbers**: `Mi calle es la número treinta.`
- **Prices**: `Cuesta veinte euros.`
- **Ages**: `Mi abuela tiene setenta y cinco años.` — remember Spanish *has* years rather than
  *is* them.

## Watch out

**Write numbers as words, not digits.** Throughout this course numbers are always spelled out —
`veinticinco`, never `25`. That is partly convention and partly practical: the audio is generated
from this text, and spelled-out numbers keep the recording aligned with the words on the page.

`uno` shortens to **un** before a masculine noun — `un euro`, not `uno euro` — and becomes
**una** before a feminine one. This is the same word you already know as "a".

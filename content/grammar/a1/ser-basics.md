Spanish has two verbs that both translate as "to be". This page is about the first one,
**ser**. You use it for things that define what something *is*: names, origins, professions,
and lasting qualities.

## The forms you need first

| Spanish | English | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **soy** | I am | Soy de España. |
| **eres** | you are | ¿De dónde eres? |
| **es** | he/she/it is | Madrid es una ciudad grande. |

## You can usually drop the pronoun

English needs "I" in "I am from Spain". Spanish does not, because **soy** already tells you the
subject is *I*. So `Soy de España` is the natural sentence, and `Yo soy de España` sounds like
you are stressing *I* specifically, as a contrast with someone else.

This is why Spanish sentences often look shorter than their English translations.

## Where you have already seen it

In *Me llamo Ana*: `soy de España`, `Madrid es una ciudad grande`, `es un gato negro`,
and the question `¿De dónde eres?`

> The other "to be" verb is **estar**, for moods, locations, and temporary states.

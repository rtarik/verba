## Numbers you need first

| | | | |
|---|---|---|---|
| uno 1 | dos 2 | tres 3 | cuatro 4 |
| cinco 5 | seis 6 | siete 7 | ocho 8 |
| nueve 9 | diez 10 | once 11 | doce 12 |

## Asking and answering

> `¿Qué hora es?` — What time is it?
> `Son las tres.` — It is three o'clock.

Notice the mismatch: the question uses singular **es**, the answer uses plural **son**. That is
because the answer is really "*they* are the three [hours]" — the hours are plural, so the verb
is too.

The one exception is one o'clock, which really is singular: `Es la una.`

## Why 'las'

Times always take the feminine plural article `las`, because `hora` is a feminine noun:
`las tres`, `las ocho`, `las once`. There is no version without it.

## Halves and parts of an hour

- `las tres **y media**` — half past three
- `las tres **y cuarto**` — quarter past three
- `las tres **y cinco**` — five past three

Spanish counts *forward* from the hour with `y`. For times approaching the next hour it counts
backwards with `menos`: `las tres **menos** cinco` is five to three.

## Saying when something happens

Use **a** for "at":

> `Mi clase empieza **a** las cuatro.`

And for the broad parts of the day, use **por**:

| | |
|---|---|
| por la mañana | in the morning |
| por la tarde | in the afternoon |
| por la noche | at night |

## Watch out

`mañana` means both "morning" and "tomorrow". `Por la mañana` is "in the morning"; `mañana` on
its own is "tomorrow". Context does all the work.

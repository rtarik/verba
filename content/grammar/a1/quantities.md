## Buying by amount

Spanish joins the measure to the thing with **de**:

- un kilo **de** tomates
- medio kilo **de** naranjas
- una docena **de** huevos
- una botella **de** agua
- un poco **de** café

The `de` never disappears and never changes. If you can say "a *something* of X" in English,
Spanish wants `de`.

## Saying how much you want

| | |
|---|---|
| mucho / mucha | a lot |
| poco / poca | little |
| todo / toda | all |
| más | more |
| menos | less |

The first three behave like adjectives and agree with what they describe:
`much**a** comida`, `much**o** dinero`, `much**as** tiendas`.

`más` and `menos` never change: `más pan`, `más verduras`.

## Comparing two things

Put **más** or **menos** around the quality, and join with **que**:

> `El mercado es **más** barato **que** el supermercado.`
> `Esta receta es **menos** difícil **que** la otra.`

For "the most", add the article:

> `Es **el** plato **más** famoso de España.`

Notice it is `de España`, not "in Spain" — Spanish uses `de` where English uses "in" for
superlatives.

## Watch out

A few comparisons have their own word instead of `más`:

- bueno → **mejor** (better), not *más bueno*
- malo → **peor** (worse), not *más malo*

`Es mejor que el supermercado` — better than the supermarket. This is the same shortcut English
makes with good → better.

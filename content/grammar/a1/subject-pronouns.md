| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| **yo** | I |
| **tú** | you |
| **él** | he |
| **ella** | she |
| **nosotros** | we |
| **ellos** | they |

## The surprising part: you can usually leave them out

Spanish verb endings already say who is doing the action, so the pronoun is normally
unnecessary:

- `Soy de España` — "I am from Spain". The `-oy` ending already means *I*.
- `Vivimos en Madrid` — "We live in Madrid". The `-imos` ending already means *we*.

Adding the pronoun is not wrong, but it adds emphasis. `Yo soy de España` leans on the *I*, as
if contrasting with someone else. Beginners often add pronouns everywhere out of English habit,
which makes every sentence sound faintly insistent.

## When they earn their place

Use the pronoun when you are drawing a contrast, exactly as *¿De dónde eres?* does:

> `Mi amigo Pablo es de México. **Él** vive en una ciudad muy grande.`

Here `él` makes clear the subject has switched from Ana to Pablo.

## Watch out for nosotros

`nosotros` means "we" for a group of men, or a mixed group. An all-female group is `nosotras`.
The same pattern applies to `ellos` and `ellas`. When a group is mixed, Spanish uses the
masculine form — even if there is only one man in it.

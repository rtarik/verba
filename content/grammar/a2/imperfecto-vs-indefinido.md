This is the hardest choice in A2 Spanish, and unlike
[he comido or comí](perfecto-vs-indefinido) you cannot settle it by looking at a time marker.
Both tenses can describe the same afternoon. What differs is **what you are doing with it**.

> The imperfecto **paints the scene**. The preterite **moves the story on**.

## The clearest example in the unit

> `**Llovía** cuando **salí**.` — It was raining when I left.

The rain is the setting: no beginning, no end, just going on. The leaving is one event at one
moment. Both are past; they are doing different jobs.

Swap them and you change the meaning, not the grammar:

| | |
|---|---|
| `**Llovía** mucho.` | It was raining — the backdrop |
| `**Llovió** mucho.` | It rained a lot — and then it stopped. A completed episode |

Neither is wrong. They are different statements.

## The film test

Ask yourself: could a camera **film** this happening, start to finish, as an event?

- **Yes** → preterite. `Sofía llegó. Miró alrededor. Me preguntó.`
- **No, it is just how things were** → imperfecto. `Hacía calor. Había mucha gente. Yo estaba sentada.`

Look at the opening of *El día que conocí a Sofía*: an entire paragraph of imperfecto — the
afternoon, the heat, where she sat, what she was reading. Nothing happens. Then `Sofía llegó`
and every verb afterwards is preterite. That shape is what a Spanish story looks like.

## Habits against single occasions

| | |
|---|---|
| `**Iba** al colegio a pie.` | every day, for years |
| `**Fui** al colegio a pie.` | one particular day |

`Todos los días`, `siempre`, `normalmente`, `cada verano` pull towards the imperfecto.
`Un día`, `ayer`, `de repente`, `entonces` pull towards the preterite.

## Always imperfecto

Some things are background by nature and rarely appear in the preterite at all:

- **age** — `Tenía ocho años.`
- **time** — `Eran las tres.`
- **weather** — `Hacía frío.`
- **feelings and opinions** — `Estaba cansada. Pensaba que...`

See [setting the scene](describing-in-the-past).

## Watch out

Four verbs change meaning depending on which past you choose:

| | imperfecto | preterite |
|---|---|---|
| conocer | knew (someone) | **met** |
| saber | knew (a fact) | **found out** |
| poder | was able to | **managed to** |
| querer | wanted | **tried to** |

`**Conocía** a Sofía` means I already knew her. `**Conocí** a Sofía` means that is when we met.
One letter of difference, an entirely different story.

The participle is the second half of `he trabajado`. Regular ones are built from the infinitive
by swapping the ending:

| verb type | ending | example |
|---|---|---|
| **-ar** | **-ado** | trabajar → trabaj**ado** |
| **-er** | **-ido** | comer → com**ido** |
| **-ir** | **-ido** | vivir → viv**ido** |

That is the whole regular system: `-ar` verbs take `-ado`, everything else takes `-ido`.

## The irregulars worth memorising

There are only a handful, but they are extremely common — you will meet all of these in Unit 5:

| verb | participle | |
|---|---|---|
| hacer | **hecho** | done, made |
| decir | **dicho** | said |
| ver | **visto** | seen |
| escribir | **escrito** | written |
| abrir | **abierto** | opened |
| volver | **vuelto** | returned |
| poner | **puesto** | put |

Note that `ser` → **sido** and `ir` → **ido** are both regular, despite those verbs being
irregular everywhere else. A small mercy.

## Accents on -er and -ir verbs

When the stem ends in a vowel, the `i` of `-ido` takes an accent so it stays a separate syllable:

- leer → le**í**do (three syllables: le-í-do)
- oír → o**í**do

Without the accent, `leido` would be read as two syllables and sound wrong.

## Participles also work as adjectives

The same word can describe a noun, and then it **does** agree in gender and number:

- `La tortilla está lista.` — ready
- `La puerta está **abierta**.` — open
- `Los correos están **escritos**.` — written

So `abierto` is "opened" after `he`, but "open" when describing a door. Same form, two jobs —
and only the adjective job makes it agree.

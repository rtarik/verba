## Comparing two things

> **más** / **menos** + adjective + **que**

- `El tráfico es **más** malo **que** en Madrid.` → actually: `es **peor que**` (see below)
- `La comida es **más** barata **que** en España.`
- `Hablan **más** rápido **que** Pablo.`

For "as ... as", use **tan ... como**: `Es **tan** grande **como** Madrid.`

## The four irregulars

Four adjectives refuse `más` and have their own comparative form:

| | |
|---|---|
| bueno → **mejor** | better |
| malo → **peor** | worse |
| grande → **mayor** | older / greater |
| pequeño → **menor** | younger / lesser |

`Más bueno` and `más malo` do exist, but they mean morally good or badly behaved — not better or
worse. For quality, always `mejor` / `peor`.

## The superlative

Add the article to the comparative:

> `**la** ciudad **más** poblada` — the most populated city
> `**el** plato **más** famoso` — the most famous dish
> `**lo mejor**` — the best part

And note the preposition: Spanish says `el más famoso **de** España`, where English says "in
Spain".

## que or de?

| | |
|---|---|
| before a **noun or pronoun** | `más grande **que** Madrid` |
| before a **number** | `más **de** veinte` |
| before a **whole clause** | `más grande **de lo que** imaginaba` |

That last one catches people out. "Bigger than I imagined" is `más grande **de lo que**
imaginaba`, never *más grande que imaginaba*.

## lo + adjective

`lo` before an adjective makes an abstract noun — "the ... part", "the ... thing":

- `**lo mejor**` — the best part
- `**lo difícil**` — the hard part
- `**lo demás**` — the rest

`Lo más difícil no fue el idioma` — the hardest thing wasn't the language.

Some things are almost never events. When you describe them in the past, Spanish reaches for the
imperfecto without hesitating — and using the preterite instead sounds distinctly odd.

## The five that are always background

| | |
|---|---|
| **age** | `**Tenía** ocho años.` |
| **the time** | `**Eran** las tres de la mañana.` |
| **weather** | `**Hacía** frío. **Llovía**.` |
| **physical description** | `La casa **era** grande. **Estaba** oscura.` |
| **feelings and thoughts** | `**Estaba** cansada. **Pensaba** que...` |

Note that time uses the plural: `**Eran** las tres`, because it agrees with `las tres`. Only one
o'clock takes the singular: `**Era** la una`.

## ser or estar, still

The `ser`/`estar` split you learned in Unit 1 carries over unchanged:

- `La casa **era** grande.` — what it was, permanently
- `La casa **estaba** oscura.` — how it was at that moment

Both in the imperfecto, because both are description. The tense answers "is this scene or
event?"; `ser` versus `estar` answers "is this identity or condition?". They are separate
questions and you answer both.

## había, not hubo

The past of `hay` is **había**, and it stays one form for singular and plural:

- `**Había** mucha gente.` — there were a lot of people
- `**Había** un problema.` — there was a problem

`hubo` exists, but it means something closer to "there occurred", for events: `Hubo un
accidente`. When you are describing a scene, you want `había`.

## Putting it together

A Spanish narrative usually opens with a block of imperfecto and then switches:

> `**Era** una tarde de septiembre y **hacía** mucho calor. Yo **estaba** sentada al lado de la
> ventana y **leía** un libro. **Había** mucha gente...`
>
> `Sofía **llegó**, **miró** alrededor y me **preguntó**...`

Everything before the switch is the set. Everything after it is the plot.

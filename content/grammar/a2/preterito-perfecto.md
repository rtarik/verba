Spanish has several past tenses. This is the one to learn first, because it is the one you hear
constantly in Spain and because it is built from parts you already have.

It takes **two words**: a form of `haber`, then a participle.

| | |
|---|---|
| **he** trabajado | I have worked |
| **has** trabajado | you have worked |
| **ha** trabajado | he/she has worked |
| **hemos** trabajado | we have worked |
| **han** trabajado | they have worked |

Only the first word changes. `trabajado` stays `trabajado` no matter who is speaking — it never
agrees with anything.

## haber is not the verb "to have"

You already know `tener` for having things. `haber` is a different verb that exists mainly to
prop up other verbs. You have met it once before, as **hay** — and that is the same verb wearing
a different hat.

So `He tenido una reunión` is "I have had a meeting": `he` is the helper, `tenido` is the real
verb. It looks odd once and then never again.

## When to use it

Use it for anything inside a stretch of time **that has not finished yet**:

- `**Hoy** he trabajado mucho.` — today is not over
- `**Esta semana** ha sido larga.` — this week is not over
- `**Este año** he ahorrado dinero.` — this year is not over

And for life experience with no date attached: `Nunca **he estado** en México.`

The giveaway is usually a time expression: `hoy`, `esta mañana`, `esta semana`, `este año`,
`ya`, `todavía`, `nunca`, `alguna vez`.

## Nothing may come between the two words

This is the rule beginners break most often. Pronouns and `no` go **in front of the whole
thing**, never in the middle:

- `Ya **la he** limpiado.` — not *he la limpiado*
- `**No he** hecho nada.` — not *he no hecho*
- `¿**Lo has** visto?` — not *has lo visto*

`he` and `limpiado` behave as a single unit. Once you hear them as one word, the order stops
being a rule you apply and starts sounding wrong when broken.

## Watch out

In much of Latin America the simple past (Unit 6) is used where Spain would use this tense.
`¿Qué has hecho hoy?` in Madrid is `¿Qué hiciste hoy?` in Mexico City. Both are correct Spanish;
this course follows peninsular usage.

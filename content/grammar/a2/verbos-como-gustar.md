`gustar` is not alone. A whole family of verbs uses the same backwards structure, where the thing
is the subject and the person is an indirect object.

| | |
|---|---|
| **encantar** | to love | `Me **encanta** el idioma.` |
| **molestar** | to annoy | `Me **molesta** el ruido.` |
| **interesar** | to interest | `Me **interesa** la historia.` |
| **parecer** | to seem | `Me **parece** difícil.` |
| **doler** | to hurt | `Me **duele** la cabeza.` |
| **importar** | to matter | `No me **importa**.` |
| **quedar** | to have left | `Me **quedan** dos días.` |
| **faltar** | to be missing | `Me **falta** tiempo.` |

All follow the Unit 4 pattern: **me / te / le / nos / les** + verb agreeing with the thing.

## The agreement trips people up

The verb matches the thing, not the person:

- `Me **duele** la cabeza.` — one head
- `Me **duelen** los pies.` — two feet
- `Me **queda** un día.` / `Me **quedan** dos días.`

## encantar has no "very"

`Me encanta` already means "I love it". You cannot say *me encanta mucho* — the verb is at
maximum already. For degrees, use `gustar`: `me gusta mucho`, `me gusta muchísimo`.

## dar + noun works the same way

Several fixed expressions build feelings out of `dar`:

| | |
|---|---|
| `Me **da** rabia.` | It infuriates me |
| `Me **da** miedo.` | It frightens me |
| `Me **da** pena.` | It saddens me |
| `Me **da** igual.` | I don't mind |
| `Me **da** envidia.` | I envy it |

Literally "it gives me rage / fear / sadness". The feeling is the subject; you receive it.

## Watch out

`parecer` splits two ways. With an indirect object it works like `gustar` — `me parece difícil`
(it seems difficult to me). Without one it simply means "to seem" — `parece cansado` (he seems
tired). Same verb, different structure.

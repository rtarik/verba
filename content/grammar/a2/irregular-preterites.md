A small group of very common verbs build this tense from a changed stem — and then all take
**the same endings**, which are not the regular ones.

## The shared endings

**-e, -iste, -o, -imos, -ieron**

Note what is missing: **no accents**. `tuve`, not *tuvé*. `hizo`, not *hizó*. This is the quickest
way to spot an irregular preterite in a text.

## The stems worth memorising

| verb | stem | yo form |
|---|---|---|
| tener | tuv- | **tuve** |
| estar | estuv- | **estuve** |
| poder | pud- | **pude** |
| poner | pus- | **puse** |
| hacer | hic- | **hice** |
| venir | vin- | **vine** |
| decir | dij- | **dije** |
| querer | quis- | **quise** |
| saber | sup- | **supe** |

Two small adjustments: `hacer` becomes **hizo** in the he/she form (a `c` before `o` would harden
the sound), and stems ending in `j` drop the `i` of the plural — **dijeron**, not *dijieron*.

## The strangest pair in Spanish

**ser** and **ir** have *identical* preterites. Not similar — identical.

| | |
|---|---|
| fui | I went / I was |
| fue | he went / it was |
| fuimos | we went / we were |

> `**Fui** a México.` — I went.
> `**Fue** un viaje largo.` — It was.

There is no way to tell them apart except by what follows. In practice this causes almost no
confusion, because "went" is followed by a place and "was" by a description. Spanish has lived
with this for centuries without complaint.

## Verbs that change meaning in this tense

A few verbs mean something different in the preterite. The most useful:

| | present | preterite |
|---|---|---|
| conocer | to know someone | **to meet for the first time** |
| saber | to know a fact | **to find out** |
| poder | to be able | **to manage to** |
| querer | to want | **to try to** |

So `Mis abuelos se **conocieron** en 1960` means they *met* in 1960 — not that they knew each
other. The tense is doing lexical work, not just temporal work.

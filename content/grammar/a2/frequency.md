The full scale, from all the time to never:

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| **siempre** | always |
| **normalmente** | normally |
| **a menudo** | often |
| **a veces** | sometimes |
| **de vez en cuando** | now and then |
| **casi nunca** | almost never |
| **nunca** | never |

## Counting the occasions

Use `vez` / `veces` with a number, and `por` for the period:

- `dos **veces** por semana` — twice a week
- `tres o cuatro **veces** al día` — three or four times a day
- `una **vez** al año` — once a year

To ask, there are two natural forms:

> `¿**Con qué frecuencia** estudias?`
> `¿**Cuántas veces** por semana estudias?`

## Where they go

Most frequency words are flexible and can open the sentence or follow the verb:

- `**Normalmente** pongo música.` / `Pongo música **normalmente**.`
- `**A menudo** estudio por la noche.` / `Estudio **a menudo** por la noche.`

`siempre` and `nunca` prefer to sit right before the verb: `**siempre** dice lo mismo`.

## nunca and the double negative

`nunca` can go before the verb on its own, or after it — but then Spanish wants `no` as well:

- `**Nunca** hablo español fuera de clase.`
- `**No** hablo **nunca** español fuera de clase.`

Both are correct and mean the same thing. The second is not a mistake — see
[ya, todavía, nunca](ya-todavia) for why Spanish is comfortable with two negatives.

## Watch out

`a menudo` only exists as that phrase. `menudo` on its own means "small" or, in exclamations,
something close to "what a...": `¡Menudo problema!` is "what a problem!" — nothing to do with
frequency at all.

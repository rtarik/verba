Instead of repeating a noun, Spanish swaps in a short pronoun. There are two sets, and telling
them apart is the whole difficulty.

## Direct: what receives the action

| | |
|---|---|
| **lo** | it / him (masculine) |
| **la** | it / her (feminine) |
| **los** | them (masculine) |
| **las** | them (feminine) |

> `¿El pasaporte? **Lo** tienes en la mano.`
> `¿Las maletas? **Las** bajé al coche.`

The pronoun matches the gender and number of the noun it replaces — not of the person speaking.

## Indirect: who receives the thing

| | |
|---|---|
| **me** | to me |
| **te** | to you |
| **le** | to him / her / you (usted) |
| **nos** | to us |
| **les** | to them |

> `A su madre **le** compré un libro.`

Notice Spanish says it twice — `a su madre` **and** `le`. That is not redundancy you should trim;
both are required. You met the same doubling with `gustar`: `A mi hermana **le** gusta`.

## Where they go

**Before** a conjugated verb, always:

> `Siempre **la** olvidas.` — not *olvidas la*

Attached to the **end** of an infinitive, gerund or command:

- `¿Puede ayudar**me**?`
- `Está enseñándo**me**.`
- `Lláma**me**.`

With `he comido` or `estoy comiendo`, the pronoun goes before the whole unit or attaches to the
end — never in the middle: `**Lo** he visto` or `Estoy viéndo**lo**`, never *he lo visto*.

## Both at once

Indirect first, then direct:

> `**se los** di` — I gave them to them

And a quirk: `le` and `les` turn into **se** before `lo/la/los/las`, because Spanish will not
tolerate two l-pronouns together. So *le lo di* is impossible; it must be `**se** lo di`.

## Watch out

Adding a pronoun to a command shifts the stress, so an accent appears in writing:
`llama` → `llám**a**me`, `enseña` → `enséñ**a**me`. The accent is not decoration — without it the
word would be read with the wrong stress.

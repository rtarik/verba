Several pairs of Spanish verbs collapse into a single English word. These are the ones that cause
the most trouble.

## saber vs conocer — both "to know"

| | |
|---|---|
| **saber** | facts, information, how to do something |
| **conocer** | people, places, being familiar with |

- `**Sé** dónde vive.` — I know the fact
- `**Conozco** a su hermana.` — I know the person
- `**Sé** cocinar.` — I know how to
- `**Conozco** Madrid.` — I'm familiar with it

Rough test: if you could add "how to" or "that...", it is `saber`. If you could add "personally"
or "my way around", it is `conocer`.

Both change meaning in the preterite, as Unit 6 covered: `**supe**` is "I found out",
`**conocí**` is "I met for the first time".

## pedir vs preguntar — both "to ask"

| | |
|---|---|
| **pedir** | to ask *for* something — to request |
| **preguntar** | to ask *a question* |

- `**Pedimos** la cuenta.` — we asked for the bill
- `Me **preguntó** si quería.` — he asked me whether I wanted to

`Pedir` takes an object you want. `Preguntar` takes a question. `Pedí una pregunta` is
nonsense — it would mean you ordered a question off a menu.

## llevar vs traer — both "to take/bring"

The difference is direction relative to the speaker:

- `**Traje** fotos.` — I brought them here, towards where I am
- `Le **llevé** una botella.` — I took it there, away from me

## quedar vs quedarse

- `**Quedé** con Diego.` — I arranged to meet him
- `Me **quedé** en casa.` — I stayed at home
- `**Quedan** dos días.` — two days are left

Three genuinely different meanings from one verb, separated only by whether it is reflexive and
what follows it.

## Watch out

`Saber` also means "to taste": `sabe bien` — it tastes good. Context separates it, but it can be
startling the first time you meet it on a menu.

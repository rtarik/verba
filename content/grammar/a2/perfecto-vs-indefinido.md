Two past tenses, and English gives you no help choosing — it often allows either. Spanish does
not. The question to ask is not *how long ago?* but:

> **Is the period of time it happened in over yet?**

| | |
|---|---|
| `**Hoy** he comido con Sofía.` | today is not finished |
| `**Ayer** comí con mi hermana.` | yesterday is finished |
| `**Esta semana** he trabajado mucho.` | this week is still running |
| `**La semana pasada** trabajé doce horas.` | that week is closed |

Note that "how recent" is irrelevant. Something ten minutes ago takes `comí` if it happened
*ayer por la noche*; something eight months ago takes `he` if it happened *este año*.

## The markers do the deciding

Learn them in two columns and the choice mostly makes itself.

| takes **he comido** | takes **comí** |
|---|---|
| hoy | ayer |
| esta mañana / semana / año | anoche |
| ya, todavía | la semana pasada |
| nunca, alguna vez | el año pasado |
| últimamente | en 2019 |
| | hace dos años |

## Why it matters more than it looks

Getting this wrong rarely makes you incomprehensible, but it makes every sentence sound slightly
off — the way "I have seen him yesterday" does in English. It is the single clearest marker of a
learner in otherwise decent Spanish.

## Watch out

**This is a Spain rule.** In most of Latin America the preterite covers both jobs, and
`¿Qué hiciste hoy?` is perfectly normal. Neither is wrong; they are different standards. This
course teaches the peninsular pattern, so if you learn Spanish from Mexican or Argentine
speakers, expect them to use `comí` where these texts use `he comido`.

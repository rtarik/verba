Spanish has a proper future tense, and in conversation people mostly ignore it in favour of this:

> `**Voy a** ir a México.` — I'm going to go to Mexico.

Three parts: **ir** + **a** + a plain verb. Only `ir` changes.

| | |
|---|---|
| **voy a** trabajar | I'm going to work |
| **vas a** trabajar | you're going to work |
| **va a** trabajar | he/she is going to work |
| **vamos a** trabajar | we're going to work |
| **van a** trabajar | they're going to work |

## The a is compulsory

`Voy trabajar` is wrong. It is always `voy **a** trabajar` — the same little `a` you have already
met in `empezar a` and `aprender a`.

And when the plain verb is `ir` itself, you get `voy a ir`. It looks like a stutter and is
entirely normal.

## When to use it rather than the future tense

Rough rule: this one is for **plans and intentions**, the future tense is for **predictions**.

| | |
|---|---|
| `**Voy a** estudiar esta noche.` | I've decided; it's the plan |
| `**Estudiaré** más el año que viene.` | I expect so; we'll see |

In practice `ir a` dominates spoken Spanish, especially for anything soon. If you are unsure
which to use, this one will almost never sound wrong.

## Spanish also uses the plain present

For things that are scheduled, the present tense is enough — as it is in English:

- `Mi jefe **se jubila** en septiembre.` — my boss retires in September
- `El tren **sale** a las ocho.` — the train leaves at eight

So Spanish gives you three ways to talk about the future, and the plainest one is often right.

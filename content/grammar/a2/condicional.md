If you know the future, you already know this tense. **Same stem, different endings** — and the
endings are the ones you learned for the imperfecto.

| | hablar | tener |
|---|---|---|
| yo | hablar**ía** | tendr**ía** |
| tú | hablar**ías** | tendr**ías** |
| él / ella | hablar**ía** | tendr**ía** |
| nosotros | hablar**íamos** | tendr**íamos** |
| ellos | hablar**ían** | tendr**ían** |

The irregular stems are **exactly** the ones from the future: `tendr-`, `podr-`, `har-`, `dir-`,
`saldr-`, and so on. Nothing new to memorise.

## What it is for

**Hypotheticals** — what would happen, in a world that is not this one:

> `**Viviría** en una casa pequeña, cerca del mar.`

**Politeness** — this is why you have been using `me gustaría` since Unit 5:

| blunt | polite |
|---|---|
| `Quiero un café.` | `**Querría** un café.` |
| `Me gusta ir.` | `Me **gustaría** ir.` |
| `¿Puedes ayudarme?` | `¿**Podrías** ayudarme?` |

The conditional softens a request the way "would" and "could" do in English. In a shop or with a
stranger it is the difference between abrupt and normal.

**Guessing about the past** — the mirror of the future's guessing job:

> `**Serían** las tres cuando llegó.` — It must have been about three.

## Watch out

English "would" also means "used to": *"When I was small I would walk to school."* That is **not**
this tense — Spanish uses the imperfecto:

> `Cuando era pequeña **iba** al colegio a pie.`

Using `iría` there would mean "I would go", as a hypothetical. Two different ideas that English
happens to spell the same way.

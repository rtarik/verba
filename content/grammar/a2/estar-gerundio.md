`estar` plus a **gerund** says that something is going on at this moment.

> `**Estoy preparando** la cena.` — I'm making dinner.

Two parts, exactly like `he comido`: `estar` changes for the person, the gerund never changes.

## Building the gerund

| verb type | ending | |
|---|---|---|
| **-ar** | **-ando** | hablar → habl**ando** |
| **-er** | **-iendo** | comer → com**iendo** |
| **-ir** | **-iendo** | escribir → escrib**iendo** |

Once again `-er` and `-ir` behave identically.

## The ones that change

Two small groups. Both changes exist to make the word pronounceable:

**A vowel before the ending turns the i into y:**

- leer → le**y**endo
- oír → o**y**endo
- creer → cre**y**endo

**Some -ir verbs change their stem vowel:**

- dormir → d**u**rmiendo
- decir → d**i**ciendo
- pedir → p**i**diendo
- corregir → c**o**rrigiendo

If you have met the preterite of these verbs, the change will look familiar — it is the same one.

## It must be estar

Never `ser`. `Soy preparando` is not a thing. This is one of the clearest jobs in the
`ser`/`estar` split: something in progress is a temporary state, and temporary states are
`estar` territory.

## Where the pronoun goes

Two options, both correct:

- `**Lo** estoy entendiendo.` — before the whole unit
- `Estoy entendiéndo**lo**.` — attached to the gerund, with an accent added

The first is more common in speech. What you cannot do is put it in the middle:
*estoy lo entendiendo*.

## Watch out

Spanish uses this tense **far less** than English uses "-ing". For anything habitual, the plain
present is correct and the continuous would sound wrong:

| | |
|---|---|
| `**Trabajo** en una oficina.` | I work / I'm working (these days, generally) |
| `**Estoy trabajando** ahora mismo.` | right this second |

And for the future English says "I'm going tomorrow", where Spanish uses the plain present or
`ir a`: `**Voy** mañana`, never *estoy yendo mañana*.

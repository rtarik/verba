You have been reading commands since the recipe in Unit 4. Here is where they come from.

## The tú command

For most verbs it is **identical to the he/she present tense**:

| | he/she says | you do it! |
|---|---|---|
| hablar | habla | ¡**Habla**! |
| comer | come | ¡**Come**! |
| girar | gira | ¡**Gira** a la derecha! |
| seguir | sigue | ¡**Sigue** recto! |

No new form to learn — just drop the pronoun and mean it.

## Eight irregular commands

These shrink to a single syllable, and they are all extremely common:

| verb | command | |
|---|---|---|
| salir | **sal** | go out |
| venir | **ven** | come |
| poner | **pon** | put |
| hacer | **haz** | do |
| decir | **di** | say |
| tener | **ten** | have |
| ir | **ve** | go |
| ser | **sé** | be |

## Pronouns attach to the end

`Llama` + `me` = `**llámame**`. `Dime` , `ponlo`, `dámelo`.

The accent appears because the added syllable shifts the stress; without it the word would be
read wrongly. This is the opposite of the usual rule, where pronouns go *before* the verb — a
command is the one place they follow it.

## The usted command is different

Polite commands do **not** use the present tense. They borrow forms from the subjunctive, which
you will meet properly in Unit 11:

| tú | usted |
|---|---|
| sal | **salga** |
| toma | **tome** |
| ven | **venga** |
| haz | **haga** |

Roughly: take the `yo` present form, drop the `-o`, and add the *opposite* vowel — `-ar` verbs
take `e`, `-er`/`-ir` verbs take `a`. `tomo` → `tome`. `salgo` → `salga`.

## Watch out

This page is only about telling someone **to do** something. Telling them **not** to is a
different form entirely — `no salgas`, not *no sal* — and it needs the subjunctive. That waits
until B1.

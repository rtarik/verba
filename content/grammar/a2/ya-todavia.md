Four small words that travel with the `he trabajado` tense. They are what makes it useful in
conversation rather than just grammatical.

| Spanish | English | |
|---|---|---|
| **ya** | already | it is done |
| **todavía no** | not yet | it is coming |
| **nunca** | never | it has not happened, ever |
| **alguna vez** | ever | asking about a whole life |

## ya and todavía no are a pair

They answer the same question in opposite directions:

> `—¿Has limpiado la cocina?`
> `—Sí, **ya** la he limpiado.` / `—**Todavía no**.`

`Todavía no` works as a complete answer on its own — you do not need to repeat the verb. It is
one of the most useful two-word replies in Spanish.

## Asking about someone's life

`alguna vez` turns a question into "have you ever...":

> `¿Has viajado **alguna vez** fuera de España?`
> `¿Has probado **alguna vez** la tortilla?`

The answer is `Sí, he...` or `No, **nunca** he...`.

## Where they sit in the sentence

`ya` and `nunca` go before the whole verb unit; `todavía` can go before or after; `alguna vez`
comes after the participle:

- `**Ya** he decidido.`
- `**Nunca** he ido a México.`
- `**Todavía** no he decidido.` / `No he decidido **todavía**.`
- `¿Has estado **alguna vez** en Francia?`

## Watch out

Spanish is comfortable with two negatives in one sentence where English is not:

> `**Nunca** he hecho **nada** especial.` — literally "I have never done nothing special."

That is correct Spanish, not a mistake. When a negative word follows the verb, Spanish wants
`no` or another negative in front of it too: `**No** he hecho **nada**.`

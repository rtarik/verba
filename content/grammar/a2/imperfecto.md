After the irregular preterites, this tense comes as a relief. It is almost perfectly regular,
and **the entire language has only three exceptions**.

| | -ar (jugar) | -er / -ir (vivir) |
|---|---|---|
| yo | jug**aba** | viv**ía** |
| tú | jug**abas** | viv**ías** |
| él / ella | jug**aba** | viv**ía** |
| nosotros | jug**ábamos** | viv**íamos** |
| ellos | jug**aban** | viv**ían** |

Once again `-er` and `-ir` share a set of endings. Every `-ía` form carries an accent, in every
person.

## The only three irregular verbs

| ser | ir | ver |
|---|---|---|
| era | iba | veía |
| eras | ibas | veías |
| era | iba | veía |
| éramos | íbamos | veíamos |
| eran | iban | veían |

That is the complete list. Compare it with the pile of irregular preterites and you will see why
this tense is the easy one.

## The yo and él forms are identical

`vivía` is both "I lived" and "he lived". Spanish tolerates this and lets context decide, exactly
as it does with `comimos`. Add the pronoun when it genuinely matters: `**yo** vivía`.

## What it is for

Three jobs, all of them about the past being *unfinished* in some way:

1. **Habits** — what you used to do, over and over
   `Todos los días **iba** al colegio a pie.`
2. **Description** — how things were, with no start or end in view
   `La casa **era** grande. **Hacía** frío. Yo **tenía** ocho años.`
3. **Actions in progress** — what was going on when something else happened
   `Mientras **esperaba**, leí los mensajes.`

English usually renders the first as "used to", the second as a plain past, and the third as
"was ...ing". Spanish uses one tense for all three.

Which one you need, and when the preterite takes over instead, is the subject of
[llovía or llovió?](imperfecto-vs-indefinido).

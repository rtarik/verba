The subjunctive of a past that never happened. Built from two things you already have:

> **imperfect subjunctive of `haber`** + **participle**

| | |
|---|---|
| yo | **hubiera** sabido |
| tú | **hubieras** sabido |
| él / ella | **hubiera** sabido |
| nosotros | **hubiéramos** sabido |
| ellos | **hubieran** sabido |

`hubiese`, `hubieses`, `hubiésemos` work identically — the same `-ra`/`-se` choice as the plain
imperfect subjunctive.

## Where it appears

**In the `si` half of a third conditional** — its main home:

> `**Si hubiera sabido**, habría insistido.`

**After a subjunctive trigger pointing at a closed past:**

| | |
|---|---|
| `Ojalá **hubiera preguntado**.` | I wish I'd asked |
| `No creo que **hubiera sido** fácil.` | I don't think it can have been easy |
| `Me molestó que no me lo **hubiera dicho**.` | it annoyed me that he hadn't told me |

Notice the last one obeys the [sequence of tenses](correlacion-de-tiempos): `molestó` is past, so
the subjunctive must be a past one too.

## The four-way contrast

Put beside the tenses you already know, the system is symmetrical:

| | present-ish | past |
|---|---|---|
| **indicative** | `he sabido` | `había sabido` |
| **subjunctive** | `haya sabido` | `hubiera sabido` |

`haya` (Unit 17) is for a recent past you are doubting or wishing about. `hubiera` is for one
that is closed and gone.

## Watch out

In speech, `hubiera` frequently replaces `habría` in the *other* half of a third conditional:
`si hubiera sabido, **hubiera** venido`. This is standard spoken Spanish across the whole
Spanish-speaking world and is not an error — but write `habría` in anything formal.

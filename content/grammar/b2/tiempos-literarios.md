Two tenses survive in Spanish that you should be able to recognise and will never need to
produce.

## Pretérito anterior — hubo terminado

Preterite of `haber` + participle. It means the same as the pluperfect, with a shade of
immediacy: an action finishing **just** before another.

> `Apenas **hubo terminado** de hablar, se levantó.`

It only ever appears after `apenas`, `en cuanto`, `cuando`, `así que` — and only in literature,
mostly nineteenth-century. Any living writer would use the plain preterite:
`en cuanto **terminó** de hablar`.

## Futuro de subjuntivo — fuere, supiere

Formed from the imperfect subjunctive with `-re` instead of `-ra`: `fuera` → `fuere`,
`supiera` → `supiere`, `hubiera` → `hubiere`.

It survives in three places and nowhere else:

- **legal texts**: `el que infringiere esta norma...`
- **proverbs**: `adonde fueres, haz lo que vieres`
- **national anthems and old formulas**

In modern Spanish its work is done by the present subjunctive or the plain present.

## Why bother at all

Because you will meet both the moment you open a novel written before about 1950, and not
recognising them is disorienting in a way that a merely unknown word is not — the verb looks
almost familiar and behaves strangely.

Recognition is worth ten minutes. Production is worth nothing.

## Watch out

`hubiere` and `hubiera` differ by one letter and belong to different centuries. If you meet
`hubiere` outside a contract, you are reading something old or something being deliberately
archaic.

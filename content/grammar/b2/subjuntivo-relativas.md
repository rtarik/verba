Every subjunctive so far has been triggered by something in the main clause — a wish, a doubt, a
conjunction. This one is different. **It depends on whether the thing exists.**

| | |
|---|---|
| `Busco un piso que **tiene** luz.` | a specific flat; I've seen it |
| `Busco un piso que **tenga** luz.` | no such flat yet; this is a specification |

The main verb is identical in both. What changes is whether the noun refers to something real.

## The test

Ask: **do I have a particular one in mind?**

- Yes → indicative. It exists, so you can describe it.
- No, I'm describing what I want → subjunctive.

`Quiero casarme con un hombre que **cocine**` — no particular man. `Quiero casarme con un hombre
que **cocina**` — this one, and he can cook.

## Compulsory after negatives

If the antecedent doesn't exist at all, the subjunctive isn't a choice:

- `No conozco a nadie que **hable** ruso.`
- `No hay nada que **podamos** hacer.`
- `No encuentro ningún piso que me **guste**.`

You can't describe something that isn't there, so Spanish uses the mood of the non-existent.

## Common in questions

Asking implies you don't know whether it exists:

> `¿Hay alguien que **sepa** arreglar esto?`

`¿Hay alguien que **sabe**...?` is possible but presupposes there is — closer to "is one of you
the person who knows?"

## Watch out

The personal `a` disappears with unspecified people, which is a useful confirmation you've got
the mood right:

| | |
|---|---|
| `Busco **a** una secretaria que **habla** francés.` | a specific person |
| `Busco una secretaria que **hable** francés.` | anyone who fits |

The `a` and the indicative travel together; drop one and you drop both.

A large part of professional Spanish consists of not quite committing.

## Attributing information elsewhere

| | |
|---|---|
| **Por lo visto** | apparently — I heard it somewhere |
| **Al parecer** | it seems — slightly more distant |
| **Según parece** | by all accounts |
| **Dicen que** | they say |

All four transfer responsibility for the claim to somebody unnamed. Useful when reporting
something you do not want to guarantee.

## Softening a conclusion

| | |
|---|---|
| **Todo apunta a que** | everything suggests that |
| **En principio** | in principle — yes, with reservations |
| **A priori** | on the face of it |
| **Diría que** | I'd say that |

**En principio** is worth flagging: in a Spanish meeting it rarely means an outright yes. It
means *yes unless something changes*, and the reservation is real.

## Softening a criticism

The most useful construction in the set:

> `**No sé si** sería mejor esperar.`

This almost never expresses genuine uncertainty. It introduces a suggestion the speaker has
already made up their mind about, while leaving room for someone else to adopt it. Related:

- `¿No sería mejor...?`
- `Quizá convendría...`
- `Yo lo veo algo ajustado.` — with `algo` doing the softening

## algo, un poco, bastante

Small quantifiers carry a lot of hedging weight. `El plazo es imposible` is a confrontation.
`El calendario parece **algo** ajustado` is the same objection, survivable in a meeting.

## Watch out

Hedging is not lying, but it can shade into it. Spanish professional culture reads heavy
qualification as competent and considerate; it reads bluntness as either very senior or very
junior. Calibrate to the room — and note that the same hedges in a personal conversation sound
evasive.

This is the rule that organises the whole subjunctive, and it is mechanical:

> **The tense of the main verb decides the tense of the subjunctive after it.**

| main verb | subjunctive | |
|---|---|---|
| present | present | `Quiero que **vengas**.` |
| future | present | `Querré que **vengas**.` |
| perfect | present | `He querido que **vengas**.` |
| **preterite** | **imperfect** | `Quise que **vinieras**.` |
| **imperfect** | **imperfect** | `Quería que **vinieras**.` |
| **pluperfect** | **imperfect** | `Había querido que **vinieras**.` |
| **conditional** | **imperfect** | `Querría que **vinieras**.` |

There is no judgement involved. Look at the first verb; the second follows.

## The mistake it prevents

`Quería que vengas` is the classic error — mixing a past main verb with a present subjunctive. It
is understood, but it marks you immediately, in the way "yesterday I go" would in English.

Learners produce it because English does not backshift subjunctives, having barely any left to
backshift.

## It governs reported speech too

> `Me pidió que lo **pensara**.`
> `Dijo que **viniéramos**.`

A past reporting verb pulls the subjunctive back with it. This is the same rule as
[reported speech](estilo-indirecto), seen from the other side, and it is what Unit 19 is about.

## The exception worth knowing

If the wish or instruction is **still in force now**, the present subjunctive can survive a past
main verb:

> `Me pidió que lo **piense** una semana.` — and I am still thinking

Compare `me pidió que lo **pensara**`, which closes the matter off in the past. Both are correct;
they differ in whether the request is still live.

This exception exists in a handful of cases and is worth recognising rather than reaching for.
The default is the table above.

## Watch out

Perfect forms follow the same logic one step down: a present main verb with a finished event
takes `haya` + participle (`No creo que **haya llegado**`); a past main verb takes `hubiera` +
participle (`No creía que **hubiera llegado**`). The second of those is Unit 20.

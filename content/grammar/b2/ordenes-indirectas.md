When you repeat something someone told you to do, the command disappears and a subjunctive takes
its place.

| what was said | what you report |
|---|---|
| `**Piénsalo.**` | `Me pidió que lo **pensara**.` |
| `**Ven** el viernes.` | `Me dijo que **viniera** el viernes.` |
| `**No te preocupes.**` | `Dijo que no me **preocupara**.` |

## Positive and negative merge

This is worth noticing. In direct speech, positive and negative commands look completely
different — `piénsalo` against `no lo pienses`. Reported, both land in the same form:

- `Me dijo que lo **pensara**.`
- `Me dijo que **no** lo **pensara**.`

Only the `no` distinguishes them. All the irregular one-syllable imperatives — `ven`, `haz`,
`pon`, `di` — vanish entirely.

## Which subjunctive?

The [sequence of tenses](correlacion-de-tiempos) decides, as always:

| main verb | subjunctive |
|---|---|
| `Me **pide** que lo piense.` | present |
| `Me **pidió** que lo **pensara**.` | imperfect |

Nothing about the order itself changes this. Only the tense of `pedir`, `decir` or `mandar`.

## The verbs that introduce them

**pedir que**, **decir que**, **mandar que**, **ordenar que**, **rogar que**, **exigir que**,
**aconsejar que**, **recomendar que**.

Remember from Unit 11 that `decir que` is two different verbs. With the indicative it reports a
fact; with the subjunctive it passes on an instruction:

| | |
|---|---|
| `Dijo que **venía**.` | He said he was coming |
| `Dijo que **viniera**.` | He told me to come |

One letter apart, and the difference between news and an order.

## Watch out

A reported order sounds noticeably softer than the original. `Ven` is blunt; `me dijo que
viniera` is almost a description. Spanish speakers exploit this — repeating an instruction back
in reported form is a recognised way of taking the edge off it, or of stalling.

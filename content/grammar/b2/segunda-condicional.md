For conditions that are **not true**:

> **si** + imperfect subjunctive → conditional

| | |
|---|---|
| `**Si tuviera** tiempo, **aprendería** el piano.` | I don't have time |
| `**Si fuera** rica, **compraría** una casa.` | I'm not rich |
| `**Si supiera**, te lo **diría**.` | I don't know |

Compare with the [real conditions](condicionales-reales) of Unit 17: `Si tengo tiempo, te llamo`
is genuinely open. `Si tuviera tiempo` says plainly that I don't.

## The order is fixed

The subjunctive goes in the `si` half. The conditional goes in the other half. **Never the
reverse.**

- `Si tuviera, iría.` ✓
- `Si tendría, iba.` ✗

You may swap the clauses around — `Iría si tuviera tiempo` — but each verb keeps its own half.
Putting the conditional after `si` is the single most audible error in B2 Spanish, and one that
some native speakers make in casual speech, which is why you will occasionally hear it.

## English marks it too, just faintly

*"If I **were** rich"* — not *was*. English keeps one surviving subjunctive form for exactly this
job, and speakers are losing it. Spanish keeps the whole system.

## The third conditional is different

`Si tuviera` is about now or the future. For a condition in the **past** that did not happen —
"if I had known" — Spanish needs the pluperfect subjunctive, which is Unit 20.

| | |
|---|---|
| `**Si tuviera** dinero, iría.` | I don't have it now |
| `**Si hubiera tenido** dinero, habría ido.` | I didn't have it then |

## Watch out

Not every `si` takes the subjunctive. When `si` means **whether**, it behaves normally:
`No sé **si viene**`, `Me preguntó **si quería**`. Only conditional `si` triggers the mood.

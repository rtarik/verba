Unit 16 covered which connector means what. This page is about **which one belongs where** — and
getting that wrong is more conspicuous than a grammar mistake, because a mistake is forgiven and
a register slip is not.

## Contrast, by formality

| spoken | neutral | written | archaic |
|---|---|---|---|
| pero | sin embargo | no obstante | empero |

`No obstante` in conversation sounds bookish. `Empero` is a nineteenth-century fossil and would
read as a joke.

## Cause

| spoken | neutral | written | legal |
|---|---|---|---|
| porque | ya que | dado que / puesto que | por cuanto |

`Como` belongs to this group too, but only at the **start** of a sentence: `Como no contestabas,
me fui.` It cannot follow the main clause.

## Consequence

| spoken | neutral | formal |
|---|---|---|
| así que | por eso | por lo tanto / por consiguiente |

## Structuring an argument

These are neutral-to-formal and carry the shape of a text:

| | |
|---|---|
| **por una parte... por otra** | on the one hand... on the other |
| **ahora bien** | that said — qualifying your *own* point |
| **es más** | what's more |
| **de hecho** | in fact |
| **en definitiva / en resumen** | all in all |

`Ahora bien` is worth distinguishing from `sin embargo`. `Sin embargo` counters someone else;
`ahora bien` qualifies what you yourself just said.

## The practical advice

Keep a mental three-column list — **spoken / neutral / written** — and pick the column before
the word. Choosing the right column is less effort than learning new vocabulary and changes the
impression far more.

## Watch out

Spanish tolerates more connectors per paragraph than English, but the limit still exists. Three
in a paragraph is comfortable; five in a row hides the argument instead of revealing it.

English has one verb for becoming and lets context do the rest. Spanish has five, and they are
not interchangeable.

| | change | typical use |
|---|---|---|
| **ponerse** | quick, temporary | moods, colours, health |
| **volverse** | lasting, involuntary | character |
| **hacerse** | chosen or gradual | professions, beliefs, wealth |
| **quedarse** | the result of a loss | states left behind |
| **convertirse en** | full transformation | literal or dramatic |

## ponerse

Sudden and passing, almost always with an adjective:

> `Se **puso** nerviosa. Se **puso** roja. Se **puso** enferma.`

Never with a noun. *Se puso médico* is impossible.

## volverse

A change of character you did not choose and cannot easily undo:

> `Se **volvió** desconfiado. Se **volvió** insoportable.`

Usually carries a judgement. `Volverse loco` is the standard way to say someone went mad.

## hacerse

Chosen, earned or gradual — and the only one that takes a **noun** freely:

> `Se **hizo** médico. Se **hizo** vegetariano. Se **hizo** rico.`

`Hacerse mayor` (to grow older) is gradual rather than chosen, which shows the category is about
process as much as intent.

## quedarse

What is left after something is removed:

> `Se **quedó** sordo. Se **quedó** sin trabajo. Se **quedó** solo.`

Note `quedarse sin` + noun — the standard way to say you ran out of something.

## convertirse en

The strongest, and always with `en` + noun:

> `El agua se **convierte en** hielo.`

Used literally for transformation, or figuratively for something dramatic: `se convirtió en un
problema`.

## Watch out

Getting these wrong rarely blocks comprehension but sounds distinctly foreign, because a native
speaker chooses among them without thinking. If unsure, `ponerse` for adjectives and `hacerse`
for nouns will be right more often than not.

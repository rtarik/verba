Almost all the conventions live in the opening and the closing.

## Opening

| | |
|---|---|
| **Estimado señor / Estimada señora** | standard formal |
| **Estimado señor Ruiz** | with a surname |
| **Muy señores míos** | recipient unknown |
| **Hola, Marta** | a colleague |

**Followed by a colon, not a comma.** `Estimada señora:` — this is compulsory in Spanish. The
comma is an English import, increasingly common and still incorrect.

## The first line

**`Le escribo para...`** is the workhorse. It states the reason immediately, which is what a
Spanish formal email expects. Others:

- `Me pongo en contacto con usted para...`
- `En relación con su correo del pasado martes...`
- `Le agradecería que...`

## Closing, by degree

| | |
|---|---|
| **Un saludo** | everyday; safe almost anywhere |
| **Saludos cordiales** | a rung more formal |
| **Atentamente** | genuinely formal |
| **Le saluda atentamente** | official letters |

`Un saludo` covers the great majority of professional email in Spain. `Atentamente` to a
colleague reads as cold.

## Useful middles

- `Adjunto le envío...` — please find attached
- `Disculpe las molestias.` — sorry for the inconvenience
- `Quedo a la espera de su respuesta.` — I look forward to your reply
- `No dude en ponerse en contacto conmigo.` — don't hesitate to contact me

## Watch out

Spanish business email is warmer than English. Openings like `Espero que te vaya todo bien` are
common even in fairly formal exchanges, and abrupt transactional messages can read as rude.
The safest calibration is to **match what you receive** — reply in the register the other person
used.

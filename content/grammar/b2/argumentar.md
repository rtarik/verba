At B2 the difficulty stops being the sentence and becomes the paragraph.

## The three-part move

The most useful structure in a Spanish disagreement:

1. **Concede** — `Tienes razón en que el sueldo importa.`
2. **Pivot** — `Ahora bien, no es lo único que importa.`
3. **Close** — `Yo, en tu lugar, esperaría.`

Conceding first is not politeness for its own sake. It builds a shared position before a
difference is placed inside it, and it makes the other person far more likely to keep listening.
Opening with the disagreement is more honest and measurably less effective.

## The shape of a written argument

| | |
|---|---|
| thesis | `Creo que...` |
| first side | `Por una parte...` |
| second side | `Por otra...` |
| objection | `No obstante...` |
| qualification | `Ahora bien...` |
| conclusion | `En definitiva...` |

A B2 text is expected to have this shape. A sequence of correct sentences joined by `y` reads as
A2 no matter how sophisticated the vocabulary.

## Softening the disagreement

Combine with the hedges from Unit 21:

- `**No sé si** sería mejor esperar.` — a decision already made
- `Yo lo veo **algo** ajustado.` — `algo` doing the work
- `**Diría que** no es tan sencillo.`

## Useful spoken formulas

These carry no meaning and hold the conversation up while you think:

| | |
|---|---|
| **Ya, bueno.** | half-agreement that ends the topic |
| **No sé yo.** | scepticism — note the inverted order |
| **Hombre, depende.** | `hombre` is a filler, said to women too |
| **Visto así.** | conceding without agreeing to anything |

None appear in textbooks; all appear constantly.

## Watch out

Spanish disagreement is more direct than English in tone but more padded in structure. Spanish
speakers will contradict you flatly and then spend three clauses softening it. Copying only the
directness, without the padding, reads as aggressive.

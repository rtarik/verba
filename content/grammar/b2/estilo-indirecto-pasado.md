Unit 15 introduced reported speech. Here is the complete system, including the subjunctive.

| direct | reported after a past verb |
|---|---|
| presente `estoy` | imperfecto `estaba` |
| pretérito perfecto `he cambiado` | pluscuamperfecto `había cambiado` |
| indefinido `cambié` | pluscuamperfecto `había cambiado` |
| futuro `iré` | condicional `iría` |
| condicional `iría` | *unchanged* |
| imperfecto `estaba` | *unchanged* |
| pluscuamperfecto `había ido` | *unchanged* |
| presente de subjuntivo `venga` | imperfecto de subjuntivo `viniera` |
| imperativo `ven` | imperfecto de subjuntivo `viniera` |

Note the pattern at the bottom: **everything already as far back as it goes stays put.** The
imperfect, the pluperfect and the conditional have nowhere further to fall.

## Reported questions

They lose their question marks, gain `si` or a question word, and backshift like everything else:

- `¿Vuelves?` → `Me preguntó **si volvía**.`
- `¿Cuándo vuelves?` → `Me preguntó **cuándo volvía**.`

Spanish often adds a `que` before the question word — `me preguntó **que** cuándo volvía`. It
means nothing, is entirely optional, and is very common in speech.

## When not to backshift

The table is a default, not a law. If what was said **is still true**, you may keep the original
tense:

| | |
|---|---|
| `Me dijo que **tiene** dos hermanos.` | he still has them |
| `Me dijo que **tenía** dos hermanos.` | correct, but hints it may no longer hold |

With general truths, backshifting actively implies doubt: *"el profesor explicó que la tierra
**era** redonda"* suggests the teacher was mistaken. Keep the present for facts that remain facts.

The same applies to future plans. `Me dijo que **viene** en primavera` keeps spring live;
`que **vendría**` distances it slightly.

## Watch out

This is one of the places where Spanish grammar stops being a rule and becomes a choice about
meaning. Both forms are correct; they say different things. Learners tend to over-apply the
table, which makes everything sound slightly more doubtful than intended.

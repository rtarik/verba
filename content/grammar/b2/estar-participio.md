Both `ser` and `estar` take a participle, and the difference is action versus result.

| | |
|---|---|
| `La puerta **fue cerrada** a las diez.` | the action — somebody closed it |
| `La puerta **estaba cerrada**.` | the state — that is how I found it |

`ser` + participle is the passive from Unit 17: it describes the event. `estar` + participle
describes the condition the event left behind.

## estar is far more common

In everyday Spanish the result matters more than the act, so `estar` wins overwhelmingly:

- `La tienda **está cerrada**.`
- `El problema **está resuelto**.`
- `La mesa **está puesta**.`
- `El asunto **está zanjado**.`

If you find yourself writing `fue` + participle repeatedly, you are almost certainly translating
from English. Reach for `estar`, the impersonal `se`, or an unspecified plural instead.

## Agreement

In both constructions the participle behaves like an adjective and agrees with the subject:

- `La carta está escrit**a**.`
- `Los problemas están resuelt**os**.`

## The three-way contrast

Put together with the previous page, the system is:

| | |
|---|---|
| `**Fue** escrita en 1920.` | the act of writing |
| `**Está** escrita a mano.` | its current condition |
| `**Se** escribió en 1920.` | the act, stated impersonally |

The middle one is by far the most frequent in speech, and the last is the natural spoken
alternative to the first.

## Watch out

A few participles have two forms, one regular and one irregular, and they split by function:

| verb | with haber | as adjective |
|---|---|---|
| freír | he **freído** | está **frito** |
| imprimir | he **imprimido** | está **impreso** |
| soltar | he **soltado** | está **suelto** |

The irregular form is the adjective. `El pescado está frito`, never *está freído*.
